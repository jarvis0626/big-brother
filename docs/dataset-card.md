The shipped model uses real LANL2015 process, router-flow and DNS records. It uses no replay fixtures, synthetic training records, binaries, attack writeups or red-team labels. The original source is LANL's [Comprehensive Multi-Source Cybersecurity Events](https://csr.lanl.gov/data/cyber1/), DOI 10.17021/1179829. LANL describes 58 days of anonymized enterprise telemetry, approximately 12GB compressed, and waives copyright and related rights to the dataset. Cite A. D. Kent, *Comprehensive, Multi-Source Cybersecurity Events*, LANL, 2015, and *Cybersecurity Data Sources for Dynamic Network Research*, 2015.

Acquisition on 2026-10-07 used the public [Taqui raw LANL mirror](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Taqui/lanl-cyber-datasets), pinned to commit d7a15d0d504051bfe6cbc3f65377830704e824cc. Its publisher states byte-identical acquisition exclusively from the official LANL source and publishes LFS full-file SHA256. We verified the schema against LANL documentation, recorded publisher hashes, and measured our prefix and decoded-text SHA256. We did **not** download full archives or independently verify full official-source equality. Official LANL2017 direct URLs returned HTTP401 and require registration; no access controls were bypassed.

Total acquired training data is12MiB compressed. Each prefix is intentionally an incomplete gzip stream; its footer and full-file checksum cannot be verified. Complete newline-terminated records were decoded with a 128MiB expansion cap per source and an 8 KiB record bound. The final partial record was excluded. Files are never executed or extracted as executable paths. Downloads validate bounded byte-range responses, resume safely and enforce a 20% free-disk reserve. Exact URLs, hashes, schema and sizes are in [lanl-manifest.json](../training/data/lanl-manifest.json).

Process records contain time, anonymized user, computer, anonymized process name and Start/End. They contain no PID or parent identifiers. Network records contain time, duration, source/destination computers and ports, protocol, packets and bytes, with no process attribution. DNS records contain time, source computer and resolved computer. Only process start/end, DNS and network destination-port category are model inputs. Identity fields group records by host and define splits; names, IPs, users and process labels are never learned features. File, Registry, process-access, command-line and process-family behavior cannot be learned from this training subset.

Training uses the common time interval 1–17,910 seconds. The first 60% is training (time<10,746) on deterministic SHA256 host buckets 0–5. Validation uses 10,746≤time<14,328 on buckets 6–7. Test uses 14,328≤time≤17,910 on buckets 8–9. These host sets and time intervals are disjoint. Within each host, records are ordered by time and stable source/line order. Nonoverlapping 24-event windows remain together; the final incomplete window is excluded. Exact duplicate raw rows are excluded; this acquisition contained none in the common interval. Training selects 10,000 of 16,894 available windows with fixed seed 1984. Validation has 979 windows on 387 hosts; test has 1,486 windows on 376 hosts. Training has 1,834 hosts. Sparse hosts without a complete window are excluded. Saved [split definitions](../training/results/splits.json) and [window identities](../training/results/window-identities.json) support leakage checks.

This sample represents the first hours of an enterprise network and favors active hosts with complete windows. It is not a random sample of the 58-day release and does not represent personal applications. Enterprise router observations differ from per-process Windows/Sysmon network observations; anonymized non-well-known ports cannot be converted back to real port numbers. Unknown ports remain a distinct category. Destination port 53 has no training observations in the selected windows; the fixed vocabulary reserves that category, without a demonstrated learned service profile.

No classification labels were acquired. LANL red-team ground truth concerns certain authentication events and would require careful joining and incomplete-label treatment; it is not applicable merely by assuming all network or process records are malicious/benign. Reported model results are prediction losses and unlabelled threshold-exceedance fractions. Precision, recall, F1, PR-AUC, false-positive rates, false alerts/hour and detection latency are unavailable.

DARPA [OpTC](https://github.com/FiveDirections/OpTC-data) was investigated first. Its release is public domain, approximately 1 TB compressed, with Windows 10 endpoint eCAR records and known [errata](https://github.com/FiveDirections/OpTC-data/blob/master/errata.md). The public Google Drive source provided an initial 4,096-byte gzip range; fresh archive requests then returned quota-exceeded pages. Three small complete OpTC-derived Parquet files from equalNull/testing-optc-sep17 (hosts 356,440,467;31,067,533bytes total) were acquired and matched publisher SHA256. Their schema matches eCAR with flattened properties, but the mirror supplies no conversion documentation. These files are retained as inspected supplemental material and are **excluded from all training and metrics**. [The inspection manifest](../training/data/manifest.json) records the provenance limit and checksums. No dataset files are bundled in the installer or portable executable.
